# Con prep - UN veto (working brief, private)

Resolution: The United Nations should abolish the permanent members' veto power in the Security Council. We are Con, speaking second.

## Core question
Which Security Council keeps more peace: one that works through the great powers (veto) or one that tries to outvote them (no veto)? Pro must show abolition produces better outcomes, not just that the veto is unfair. We concede unfairness up front and win on outcomes: abolition either fails (great powers ignore or leave) or backfires (UN on a collision course with a nuclear power).

## Framework (one line for the judge)
The UN's first job is peace among the powers that can destroy each other; judge the round on which world keeps the great powers inside a working system. Justice arguments matter only if the institution survives to deliver them.

## Contention 1 - Keeping the giants in the room
- Uniqueness: The League failed with the great powers outside it [C1]. The UN has kept all of them inside for 80 years.
- Link: The veto was the price of US membership [C2]; great powers will not accept binding authority without it, so no veto = no Security Council "in a recognizable form" [C4]. Exit is not hypothetical: with the veto intact, the US decided to leave 31 UN-linked entities in Jan 2026 [C5]. Remove the veto and the Council becomes the next body that "no longer serves" a great power.
- Impact: The Council's consensus work stops, above all peacekeeping, which research ties strongly to peace [C6]. Nothing Pro wants can be passed or enforced by a Council the powers have left.

## Contention 2 - The safety valve
- Story: ICJ binding order to Russia (March 2022) - rejected, no means of enforcement [C9].
- Link: Without the veto, 9 votes can impose binding measures / authorize force against a nuclear power; Art. 25 obliges every member to carry decisions out [C7]. When the target refuses (as Russia did with the court), the UN either backs down (orders are empty) or states enforce against a nuclear power.
- Impact: The veto exists because "no structure of peace could be viable if war could be authorized contrary to the expressed wishes of one of the world's policemen"; the system reduces "the likelihood that major war will break out among them" [C3]. Great-power war is the largest-magnitude, irreversible impact.
- Honesty note: the hypothetical must always be labeled as a hypothetical. Authorizing force is not the same as obliging it; say "binding measures" for Art. 25 and "authorize force" separately.

## Blocks (Con 2 - the Surgeon: restate their chain, name the step that fails)

**A. Veto paralyzes the Council on Syria / Ukraine / Gaza / Sudan -> atrocities.**
Their chain: veto blocks resolution -> no action -> atrocities continue; abolish -> resolution passes -> atrocities stop. Cut at "resolution passes -> atrocities stop."
- Defense (solvency): a ruling or vote is not compliance. Russia rejected a binding ICJ order; the ruling "lacks any real means to enforce it" [C9]. The Council has no army; enforcement depends on member states, including the great power being targeted.
- Defense (non-unique): the GA already acts when the Council is blocked - Syria accountability mechanism (IIIM) [C8].
- Turn: abolition drives great powers out [C4, C5], so even the resolutions that pass today (peacekeeping [C6]) stop.
- Concede: these are real tragedies. Patrick himself says the system "fails those caught in the middle" - don't deny it; weigh it.

**B. Veto is undemocratic / unfair (5 of 193).**
- Turn: majority rule is not automatically more just. With no veto, Russia could have gathered eight others to validate the Crimea annexation, or the US eight others to deny a Palestinian state [C10]. A 9-of-15 vote of governments is not democracy either.
- Weigh: legitimacy without survival is empty [C4].

**C. Veto use is rising (Pro may read SCR: 2024 had the most vetoed drafts since 1986).**
- Defense: rising vetoes reflect rising great-power tension; abolition removes the veto, not the tension - it moves the fight outside the Council [C5].
- Turn to our C2: higher tension makes a safety valve more important, not less [C3].
- If they read SCR: the same page says 2025 saw four vetoes, down from eight - point that out using their card only.

**D. Great powers would never leave - they benefit too much.**
- They already do, with the veto intact: US leaving 31 UN-linked entities [C5]. Hull 1944 [C2]. League precedent [C1]. Bosco: the powers' condition is certainty their interests aren't prejudiced [C4].

**E. Fiat: if the resolution passes, the P5 have consented, so they won't leave.**
- Fiat gives the amendment, not future governments' behavior. The test comes the first time nine votes go against a great power's core interest. The US once signed up to the bodies it is leaving now [C5].

**F. The League had a unanimity rule (everyone had a veto) and still failed - so vetoes don't keep powers in.**
- Our card never said the veto alone guarantees membership; it says the powers left or never joined [C1]. The UN's design pairs binding enforcement with a great-power veto; the League had neither real enforcement nor the US. The claim we defend: without the veto, the powers won't accept binding authority [C2, C4].

**G. Veto shields allies from accountability (US-Israel, Russia-Syria).**
- Same cut as A: a vote against a P5 member or its ally still depends on that power's compliance [C9]; and majority votes can shield aggression too [C10].

## Weighing
- Magnitude + reversibility: war between nuclear powers [C3] outweighs regional harms, and cannot be undone.
- Prerequisite: Pro's resolutions need a Council the great powers still sit in and obey; we win that first [C4, C5]. Their impact cannot happen unless ours is solved.
- Probability: our link has happened (League [C1], US exits in 2026 [C5], Russia rejecting the ICJ [C9]); their solvency needs a great power to obey a vote it lost - no evidence that happens.

## Collapse plan
- Default: go for Contention 1 (giants in the room) in summary - best evidenced (C1, C2, C4, C5, C6), clearest for the lay judge, and it doubles as a turn on Pro's paralysis offense. Pair with C9 as the solvency takeout on Pro's case. Use C2 (safety valve) as weighing (magnitude) even if not fully extended.
- If Pro spends their rebuttal on C1 (fiat / League unanimity), collapse to C2 + C9: a vote cannot force a nuclear power, and trying to enforce it risks the war the veto exists to prevent.

## Crossfire targets (Con 1 vs Pro 1)
- "If the Council had passed a Ukraine resolution in 2022, what would have made Russia comply, when it ignored the ICJ?"
- "Which great power do you think would stay in a Council that can outvote it on sanctions and war?"
- "Does any of your evidence show a great power obeying a UN decision it opposed?"
- Concede freely: the veto is unfair and has blocked action on real atrocities. Don't concede that abolition would produce compliance.
